# Genre: Academic / competition essay

Use problem–evidence–thesis–alternatives–mechanism–feasibility–implication, **not** forced IMRaD. An essay can be theoretical or evidence-driven; do not invent surveys, experiments or “hasil penelitian”. Determine whether organizer expects argument, solution proposal, policy essay or research-style essay; follow actual rubric.

For solution essays, connect `status quo → bottleneck → root cause → evidence → proposed intervention → plausible mechanism → implementation → risk/ethics → measurable evaluation plan`. A figure can display a decision mechanism, technology architecture, scenario matrix, stakeholder map or documented trend. Only show quantitative impact as *forecast under assumptions* or actual observed field result.

For current statistics, inspect primary issuer, date, region, indicator definition; avoid sensational percentages. Compare nearest cases and literature through an evidence table; cite explicitly in prose. The winning essay is not the one with most frameworks or visuals; it is the one whose argument survives alternative explanations and is usable by its target audience.

Suggested outline: precise title; background/urgency; evidence-based problem framing; research/argument gap; thesis or idea; solution mechanism and stakeholder workflow; comparative precedents; implementation/feasibility; evaluation/risks; conclusions; references; appendix only if rules allow.
